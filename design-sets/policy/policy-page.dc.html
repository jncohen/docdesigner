Policy Brief No. 47

Policy implications

If the wealth returns to education are gated at two credentials, then the marginal public dollar buys the most where it moves a student across one of those gates. Support that stops short of completion — a year of college without a degree — sits on the flat part of the staircase and yields little in measured wealth.

This reframes a long-running debate about access versus completion. Expanding enrollment matters only insofar as it converts into finished credentials; otherwise it moves families along a segment of the curve where the slope is near zero.

“Funding enrollment without completion pays for the flat part of the staircase.”

Recommendations

  1. 1 Tie aid to completion. Structure grants and subsidies so that the largest transfers land on crossing the high-school and bachelor's thresholds.
  2. 2 Fund the last mile. Direct retention and completion resources to students within reach of a credential, where marginal wealth effects are largest.
  3. 3 Measure wealth, not just enrollment. Evaluate programs against credential attainment and downstream net worth rather than seats filled.

Notes

1. Analysis of the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances; estimates net of age, race, and marital status.

2. Threshold shares computed from the survey's replicate-weight design. Full methods available on request.
