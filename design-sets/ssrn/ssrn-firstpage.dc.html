Meridian Economics Working Paper Series · No. 31428

Cross-Sectional Differences in Wealth by Education: Measurement and Theory Alternatives

Joseph N. Cohen*  Meridian University
This draft: June 23, 2026

Abstract. This paper examines the relationship between educational attainment and household wealth in the United States using the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances. Net of age, race, and marital status, most detectable structure is captured by two credential benchmarks — high-school and bachelor's-degree completion — while finer gradations contribute little to model fit.

JEL: D31, I24, J24  ·  Keywords: household wealth, educational attainment, credentials, SCF

1.  Introduction

The positive association between education and wealth is among the most robust findings in empirical economics. Its functional form, however, remains contested. This paper asks whether the wealth return to schooling is best modeled as continuous in years or as a step function in credentials, and argues that the choice has consequences for both measurement and interpretation.

Human-capital theory (Becker, 1964; Mincer, 1974) predicts a continuous return: each year of schooling augments productivity and, in turn, lifetime accumulation. Signalling and credentialist accounts (Spence, 1973; Collins, 1979) predict discontinuities at degree completion, where credentials sort workers into distinct opportunity structures.
