Cross-Sectional Differences in Wealth by Education: Measurement and Theory Alternatives

Joseph N. Cohen
Meridian University
This draft: June 23, 2026
Abstract

This paper examines the empirical relationship between educational attainment and household wealth in the United States using the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances. We test whether the wealth return to schooling is continuous in years or concentrated at discrete credential thresholds. Net of age, race, and marital status, most detectable structure is captured by two benchmarks — high-school and bachelor's-degree completion — while finer gradations contribute little to model fit. The pattern is robust across the survey's replicate-weight design and is consistent with credentialist and institutional-sorting accounts of stratification. We discuss implications for the measurement of educational attainment in wealth models.

Keywords: household wealth, educational attainment, credentials, Survey of Consumer Finances
