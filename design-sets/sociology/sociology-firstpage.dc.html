American Journal of Social Research 763

Cross-Sectional Differences in Wealth by Education

Measurement and Theory Alternatives
Joseph N. Cohen
Meridian University

Abstract  This article examines the relationship between educational attainment and household wealth in the United States using the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances. Net of age, race, and marital status, most detectable structure is captured by two credential benchmarks — high-school and bachelor's-degree completion — while finer gradations contribute little to model fit. The pattern is consistent with credentialist and institutional-sorting accounts of stratification, and is robust across the survey's replicate-weight design.

Introduction

Few regularities in social stratification are as well established as the association between education and wealth. The better educated accumulate more; the wealthy invest more in schooling. What remains contested is the functional form of that association — whether the wealth return to education is continuous in years of schooling, or concentrated at the completion of major credentials.

This distinction maps onto competing theoretical traditions. Human-capital theory anticipates a continuous return, as each year of schooling adds market-valued skills. Credentialist and signalling theories anticipate thresholds, as degrees sort individuals into discretely different opportunity structures.

The Threshold Hypothesis

If credentials rather than accumulated instruction govern access to wealth-building positions, then the wealth distribution should exhibit discontinuities at high-school and bachelor's completion that exceed what any single additional year of schooling could produce.

We formalize this by entering credential dummies alongside a continuous years term and testing whether the thresholds absorb the association.

Data and sample.  The Survey of Consumer Finances is a triennial, nationally representative study of household balance sheets, with an oversample of high-wealth households and a replicate-weight design suited to inference about the upper tail.
