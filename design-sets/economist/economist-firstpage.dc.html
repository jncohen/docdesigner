Menacing Taiwan

Too close for comfort

NEW YORK
China is sending near-daily sorties near Taiwan, raising fears of a crisis
BY MAYA R. LINDQVIST

It was a deliberate provocation, patriotically timed. On the mainland's national day, dozens of fighters and bombers pressed toward the island, the largest single-day incursion the defence ministry had logged that year. The aircraft entered the air-defence identification zone, a buffer whose edges trigger alerts long before any territorial line is crossed.

Over the days that followed the sorties did not stop; they multiplied. Jets were scrambled, warnings broadcast, radar locks maintained. A retired air-force officer called it the tensest stretch he had seen in four decades of service.

Officials abroad were quick to register alarm. A meeting in Europe between the two powers' most senior diplomats produced the usual communiqués and the usual denials, but little reassurance. State media described the flights as routine training of no consequence.

The identification zone extends well beyond the island's coast, and in recent months the aircraft have probed its southern reaches most persistently, skirting the unofficial line down the middle of the strait.

By treating ever-larger formations as unremarkable, a rival can wear down the defenders' vigilance and blur the distinction between an exercise and the opening move of something graver.

Assessing intent is the hard part. Sabre-rattling tends to intensify whenever the island is judged to be drifting toward a more permanent separation, or whenever its friends abroad grow conspicuously warmer.

For now the flights remain flights. But each near-daily sortie narrows the margin for miscalculation, and it is miscalculation, more than design, that the island's planners have come to fear most.
