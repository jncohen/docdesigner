The view from Taipei

On the island itself the mood is harder to read than the headlines suggest. Officials speak of vigilance rather than alarm, and the daily tallies have become a fixture of the morning briefings — counted, mapped, and filed away with a practised calm.

Analysts caution against reading each incursion as a discrete signal. The value of the exercise, they argue, lies precisely in its repetition: a rhythm that dulls the response it once provoked.

"It is miscalculation, more than design, that the island's planners have come to fear most."

A RETIRED AIR-FORCE OFFICER

The ministry's own records make the escalation legible. Incursions clustered around politically charged dates, then settled into a steadier, higher baseline over the autumn.

Reading the pattern

Stripped of narrative, the tallies are just a table — but the shape of the table is the argument. A simple grouping of the daily records surfaces the clustering that the prose only gestures at.

sorties |>
  group_by(month) |>
  summarise(n = n(), peak = max(daily)) |>
  arrange(desc(n))

What the code makes plain, the diplomacy has yet to resolve. For now the flights remain flights — and the margin for error, a little narrower with each one.
