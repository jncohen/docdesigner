Estimating Neighborhood Effects under Partial Population Interference

Joanna R. Vance1  ·  Daniel Okafor1,2  ·  Priya Menon2
1Department of Sociology, Meridian University   2Center for Population Research
July 11, 2026
Abstract

Standard estimators of neighborhood effects assume that a household's outcome depends only on its own treatment. We relax this to partial interference, allowing spillovers within but not across neighborhoods, and derive a two-stage estimator that remains consistent under moderate exposure mismatch. Applying it to a large residential-mobility experiment, we find direct effects roughly one-third smaller than the naive benchmark, and non-negligible spillover onto untreated neighbors.
