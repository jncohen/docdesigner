Methods

Data. Estimates use the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances, a triennial cross-section of household balance sheets with a dual-frame sample and an oversample of high-wealth households drawn from statistics of income. All estimates apply the survey weights and are repeated across the five implicates of the multiply-imputed public file.

Model. The dependent variable is the inverse-hyperbolic-sine transform of net worth. Credential thresholds enter as indicators alongside a continuous years-of-schooling term; controls comprise a cubic in age, race and marital status. Standard errors are computed from the 999 replicate weights.

fit <- svyglm(
  ihs_networth ~ hs + ba + grad + years
    + poly(age, 3) + race + married,
  design = scf_rep )

Inference. Threshold magnitudes are compared using the bootstrap distribution over replicate weights; the reported ordering is stable in more than 98% of resamples. Reporting follows the journal's checklist for survey-weighted estimation.

Code and replicate-weight scripts are archived at the repository DOI; the SCF microdata are public and unrestricted.
