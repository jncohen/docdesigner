Credentials and the Architecture of Advantage

Joseph N. Cohen
Meridian University

The association between education and household wealth is among the most durable regularities in social stratification, yet its functional form remains contested. Drawing on the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances, this article asks whether the wealth return to schooling is continuous in years of instruction or concentrated at the completion of major credentials. Net of age, race, and marital status, most detectable structure is captured by two benchmarks—high-school and bachelor’s-degree completion—while finer gradations contribute little to model fit. The pattern is robust across the survey’s replicate-weight design and is consistent with credentialist and institutional-sorting accounts of the wealth distribution.
